The core reading

The calls

A call is BRAIN's reading of which side the data leans toward at a set moment in the round. It's a measurement, not an instruction.

Early · 4:00

The first reading, 4 minutes in. More time left means more can still change.

Sealed · 8:00

The main reading, locked at 8 minutes. It's the one tracked hardest in the record.

Late call

Shows up only when the sealed reading sat out a toss-up and price later made a clear move.

Two numbers on every call
You seeIt tells you
BRAIN confidence · 84%How strongly BRAIN leans to that side when the call locks.
Market at lock · 72%What the crowd had on that side at the same moment. Side by side, they show whether BRAIN and the crowd agreed.
Confidence tiers
SUPERBRAIN's strongest readings, with a strong record from similar rounds behind them.
GOODA clear lean. A choppy round or mixed signals can drop a reading a tier.
WEAKA lean, but close. Most misses happen here (see the scorecard on page 4).
Tags you may see

80% SETUP

The round looks like a setup that has been right 80% of the time or more in BRAIN's testing.

REVERSAL

A rare call on the underdog side, made only when the momentum supports a turn. It pays more when right, so it has its own column in the record.

Held the early call

The 8:00 reading wasn't sure enough to switch sides, so it kept the 4:00 side.

Sealed call switched

The 8:00 reading landed on the other side from the 4:00 one. The voice says it out loud.

What the record shows

Later is right more often

By 8:00 price has usually moved, so the sealed reading is right more often. The crowd sees that move too, so its odds are higher by then as well.

Tier beats timing

A SUPER reading at 4:00 has scored nearly as well as one at 8:00, and far better than any WEAK reading.

WEAK means close

Close rounds tend to stay close. 57–68% isn't far from a coin flip.

When 4:00 and 8:00 disagree

Round history keeps a running count of which reading turned out right when the two landed on different sides.

Past and tested results don't predict future results. The live record on the dashboard is always the latest word.

Flip radar

A 0–99% read on how likely price is to cross back against the call before the round ends.

It shows one reason at a time, the thing that matters most right now: big money moving against the call, or momentum turning.

WATCH21–45%. Worth keeping an eye on.
HIGH46–70%. Real pressure to turn.

Live alerts

Flip watch & voice

Alerts tell you the moment something changes, so you don't have to stare at every number.

Flip watch pills
BTC▲ UPwith call74%HOLDING BTC▼ DOWNagainst call63%TURNED XRP▲ UPagainst call55%

Slim pills float at the top of the screen, but only once an early or sealed call is in.

Pink = the market is holding with the call. Reddish pink = moving down against it. Reddish blue = moving up against it.

TURNED appears when the market has swung clearly (60%+) against the call.

What the voice says
You hearWhen
"New round" + the trendAt the start of a round, with the up / down trend on the timeframes that aren't flat.
The call + a chimeWhen the early, sealed or late call locks. "Sealed call switched" if 8:00 landed on the other side.
Whale alertsBig buys or sells that matter to the round. Before a call is in, this is almost all the voice says.
"Possible flip up / down"Only when the flip radar is high or climbing fast and price is really moving.
"Flipping" / "flipped"Only for a real flip: the crowd has moved to the other side at 60/40 or wider.
Minutes leftOnly in the last 3 minutes.
"Some traders cash out here"The call's side is at 90%+ with 2+ minutes left. It describes the market, it's not a suggestion.

Voices

Ava (calm and natural) or Sarge (a shouting drill sergeant). Pick one in Sound settings. Each browser needs one tap to turn sound on.

Sounds

A cha-ching means a whale buy just hit. A double beep means a whale sell.

The voice is built to stay calm: no repeated lines, nothing every minute, and it never reads out numbers it isn't sure about.

What's on the chart

EMA lines

Smoothed averages of recent price. Price riding above them leans up; below leans down. When two lines cross, the trend may be changing.

RSI

Momentum from 0 to 100. Above 70 is stretched to the upside, below 30 stretched to the downside. Around 50 is neutral.

MACD

Shows momentum speeding up or slowing down. A cross of its two lines is an early hint the push is changing direction.

Support & resistance

Price zones where buyers (support) or sellers (resistance) have stepped in before. Watch whether price holds a zone or gets rejected by it.

Chart patterns

BRAIN marks well-known shapes on the chart as they form and shows how often each has played out in its testing. Tap "What do these mean?" under the chart for a plain-English guide to every line and pattern.

Accountability

The record

Every call is graded against how the round actually ended. Wins and losses both stay on the screen.

Reading the result circles

Each circle tells you two things. The color is how the round ended (green = UP, red = DOWN). The letter is whether BRAIN was right: a white W when it was, an L in the circle's color when it wasn't.

WCalled UP, ended UPWCalled DOWN, ended DOWN
LCalled UP, but ended DOWNLCalled DOWN, but ended UP
Example run: WWWLWWLW

Streaks & scoreboard

The current run of right or wrong calls, the best and worst runs, and today's record at a glance. Reversal calls get their own column.

Round history

Tap the badge at the top right for today, this week and all time: the win rate for each call and tier, and every graded round.

How BRAIN keeps learning

After every round, BRAIN checks its calls against the real result. Recent rounds count more than old ones, so it adjusts quickly when conditions change.

Any change has to prove itself on rounds it has never seen before it goes live. If it doesn't make the calls better, it isn't used.

Every result is checked against the market's own final settlement, and any grade that doesn't match is corrected.
