What BRAIN is (and isn't)

BRAIN is an information service. Its software watches public market data around the clock, measures it, and shows you what it sees, live.

What BRAIN does

Gathers prices, trades and order books from the exchanges that matter, plus the prediction market's own prices.

Shows its readings live and grades every one against how the round really settled.

What BRAIN doesn't do

It doesn't give financial advice or tell anyone to buy, sell, hold or place money anywhere.

It doesn't know your finances and doesn't trade for members. Every decision is yours.

How a 15-minute round works

PieceWhat it means
The line (target)The price the market sets when the round opens. The question is simply: will the price be above or below this line when the round ends?
UP / DOWNUP = settles above the line. DOWN = settles below it.
The market %The market's own price for a side, read as the crowd's odds. 72% on UP means UP costs about 72¢ and pays $1 if right.
SettlementRounds settle on an independent index of major exchanges, averaged over the last 60 seconds, so a spike in the final seconds barely moves it.
PayoutA correct side pays $1 per contract at settlement; a wrong side pays $0. Buying at 72¢ means 28¢ profit if right and 72¢ lost if wrong, before fees.
FeesThe market charges a trading fee, largest near 50¢ and smallest near 1¢ or 99¢. Fees mean a side can be right most of the time and still lose money overall.
Before the endPrices move all round. A position can be sold before settlement at whatever the market is paying at that moment, which may be more or less than it cost.
The clock15 minutes per round. BRAIN reads it at the 4:00 and 8:00 marks and counts down continuously.
  Prediction markets carry a high risk of loss. Everything in this guide explains what BRAIN's screens show. None of it is a suggestion to trade.

The core reading

The calls

A "call" is BRAIN's model reading of which side the data leans toward at a fixed moment. It's a measurement, not an instruction.

Early · 4:00

The first reading, 4 minutes in. More time left means more can change.

Sealed · 8:00

The main reading, locked at 8 minutes. It's the one tracked hardest in the record.

Late call

Only when the sealed reading sat out a coin flip and price later clearly decided.

Two numbers on every call

Line in the boxWhat it tells you
BRAIN confidence: 84%How strongly BRAIN's own models lean to that side at the lock.
Market at lock: 72%What the market was charging for that side at the exact moment the call locked. Comparing the two shows whether BRAIN and the crowd agreed.
Market agrees · 70%The market's price for that side right now. "Agrees" means the market still favors BRAIN's side; "disagrees" means it has swung the other way.

Confidence tiers

TierConfidenceNotes
SUPERHighestThe strongest readings, backed by a strong record from similar rounds.
GOODClear leanA clear lean. A choppy round or mixed signals can lower a reading's tier.
WEAKCloseA lean, but close. This is where most misses happen (see the scorecard on page 5).

Tags you may see

80% SETUP: the round matches a pattern that scored 80%+ on rounds it was never built from. REVERSAL: the reading sides with the underdog because several signals point to a turn. Top traders agree: verified high-record traders were on the same side (sealed call only, never changes the direction). Held the early call: the 8:00 reading kept the 4:00 side because switching at that confidence has lost more often than it won (page 4).

Under the hood

How BRAIN learns

BRAIN doesn't just read the round in front of it. It checks how every past round from the same spot actually ended, wins and losses, and keeps updating.

Price-target history

At 4:00 and 8:00 BRAIN looks at where price sits against the line and what the market is doing, then checks how past rounds in a similar spot actually ended. The reason under the call shows it in plain words, e.g. "38 of 45 rounds from this spot ended UP (84%) with the trend up."

A few lucky rounds can't swing a reading on their own; it takes a real record.

Trend

BRAIN reads the short-term trend as up, down or flat.

If readings that go against a running trend keep losing, BRAIN learns that and leans with the trend instead.

Learning from losses

Recent rounds count more than older ones, so this week's misses reshape the next reading quickly.

It refreshes several times a day, and changes only go live after they prove themselves on rounds they were never built from.

Early vs sealed: the flip check

Sometimes the 8:00 reading disagrees with the 4:00 reading. BRAIN studies every past round where that happened and learns how sure the 8:00 reading has to be before switching sides has actually paid off. Below that level, the sealed reading holds the early side and says so on screen.

This rule is tested the same way and only used when it has produced more correct readings, not fewer.

What the record shows

Later is more often right, but costs more. By 8:00 price has usually moved, so the 8:00 reading is right more often, but the market already prices that in. The leading side cost about 78¢ at 8:00 vs 69¢ at 4:00, so each correct reading is worth less.

Tier matters more than timing. A 4:00 SUPER reading (86.1%) has scored nearly as well as an 8:00 SUPER (89.4%), and far better than any WEAK reading.

WEAK is where the misses are. Close rounds stay close; 57–68% isn't far from a coin flip.

Fees change the picture. In the $100 simulation (page 9), after the market's fee and a cent of slippage, most readings came out near break-even. 4:00 SUPER readings were slightly positive (+0.7% per dollar).

Past and simulated results don't predict future results. These numbers update as the backtest reruns; the live record is always on the dashboard.

Cash-out level note

When the called side reaches 90%+ on the market with 2+ minutes left, the voice says "Some traders cash out here." It describes the market; it's not a suggestion.

Late coin flip

With 3 minutes or less left and the market at 40–60%, BRAIN says the round is a real coin flip, and adds "it's flipping" if price just crossed the line.

Big money

Whales, exchanges and Trader Radar

Not every big trade matters equally. BRAIN scores each one.

Exchange trust tiers

TierExchangesWhy
IndexThe exchanges the settlement index is built fromThey feed the index the round settles on, so their trades move the settlement price directly.
Big globalThe largest global exchanges and futures marketsLead price discovery but don't count toward settlement.
Low-trustDropped from BRAINOffshore venues with enforcement history or thin volume aren't read at all.

Whale impact score (0–100)

Each big trade is scored on how much it could actually move this round, not just its size. A large buy when the round is a toss-up and price sits right on the line scores high; the same buy when the market is already at 97% scores low.

After every alert BRAIN checks whether price really moved the whale's way and keeps a running hit rate, so the score's accuracy is measured, not assumed.

Trader Radar

Tracks positions that well-known prediction-market traders show publicly, only from accounts locked as the real ones. Lookalike names (M0GS, mogs_, letters from other alphabets) are ignored as impostors. Records are kept by round, hour, day and 7 days.

Accountability

The record

Every reading is graded against how the round actually settled. Wins and losses both stay on the screen.

Streaks & scoreboard

The current run of right or wrong readings, the best and worst runs, today's record and the latest results as small circles.

Round history

Tap the badge at the top right for today, this week and all time: win rate per call, by tier, how readings did with vs against the trend, and every graded round.

Reading the result circles

Two things in one circle: the color is how the round ended (green = UP, red = DOWN), and the letter is whether BRAIN's reading was right: a white W when it was, an L in the circle's color when it wasn't.

WRead DOWN, ended DOWNWRead UP, ended UP
LRead DOWN, but ended UPLRead UP, but ended DOWN

The $100-a-trade example

To show what a reading's record looks like in dollars, BRAIN replays every graded call as if $100 had been placed at the market's price at the moment of the call, held to settlement, with the market's fee and a cent of slippage included.

This is a simulation. It uses past data, assumes fills at the price shown, and doesn't reflect real trading. Past and hypothetical results never predict future results, and nothing here suggests placing $100, or any amount, on anything.

The backtest

Several times a day BRAIN replays recent Bitcoin rounds to check which readings held up and where they lost, and refreshes what it has learned (page 4).
